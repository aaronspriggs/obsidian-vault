# Checked the UniFi UP AirQuality sensor for Home Assistant use

No system changes. Aaron asked about the [UP-AirQuality](https://store.ui.com/us/en/category/physical-security-sensors-alarms/products/up-airquality) ("Vape Detection & Air Quality Sensor") after we compared the ecobee and Dyson readings and found Poppy's room has no humidity sensor.

**Specs** ([tech specs](https://techspecs.ui.com/unifi/physical-security/up-airquality)): $99. PoE only (37–57 V, 2.2 W, 10/100 RJ45, no Wi-Fi or Bluetooth). It measures temperature (±1 °C), humidity (0–90 %, **±8 % RH**), CO₂ (±50 ppm + 3 %), PM1/2.5/4/10, a VOC index, a NOx index, an AQI and a vape index. It needs Protect 7.1+. Indoor only, Ø93 × 40.5 mm.

**Home Assistant: no readings.** The [unifiprotect docs](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/unifiprotect/) say Protect reports no sensor capabilities for the UP Air Quality, so HA gets only general device entities. Protect's public API carries the readings only inside threshold events. uiprotect won't add private-API features ([uiprotect #1007](https://github.com/uilibs/uiprotect/issues/1007), declined 2026-06-19). The request is open in [uiprotect #1154](https://github.com/uilibs/uiprotect/issues/1154) (`wait-for-ui`) and [core #178669](https://github.com/home-assistant/core/issues/178669), with no timeline as of 2026-11-22. Community workarounds exist, all on the private API with a local UniFi OS username and password (read 2026-11-22). [Tommo-101/UPAQ-MQTT](https://github.com/Tommo-101/UPAQ-MQTT) is a Docker bridge from Protect's bootstrap and WebSocket to MQTT discovery. It has 30 stars, was last pushed 2026-06-20, and exposes every reading plus LED and night-mode controls. [angristan/ha-unifi-protect-air-quality](https://github.com/angristan/ha-unifi-protect-air-quality) is a custom component that polls `/proxy/protect/api/sensors` every 30 s. It has 1 star, all its commits are from 2026-10-03, and it says Protect updates can break it; on firmware 1.0.16 NOx reads null.

**Takeaway:** not a fit while HA can't read it. For Poppy's room in particular, the ±8 % RH accuracy is too coarse to hold a 40–45 % target, and it needs a cable run. Re-check #1154 if Aaron still wants one.

## Options with a screen that fit this setup (same day)

Aaron then asked for well-reviewed sensors with a display that work with HA here. What fits: ZHA, and Matter over Thread, which already works (two IKEA MYGGSPRAY and the U400 lock). Bluetooth sensors (Aranet4, SwitchBot) are out until HA has a working adapter or proxy: the only one, on the wall panel Pi, is in `setup_retry`.

- **IKEA ALPSTUGA**, $29.99, Matter over Thread, mains-powered Thread router: CO₂, PM2.5, temperature and humidity, always-on display. Sensirion SEN63C inside per a forum post. CO₂ reviewed well; owners report temperature reading about 4 °F high with no calibration, so its humidity may read low. 3.7/5 from 253 US reviews ([IKEA US](https://www.ikea.com/us/en/p/alpstuga-air-quality-sensor-smart-70609396), [Matter Alpha](https://www.matteralpha.com/review/ikea-alpstuga-review-matter-air-quality-monitor)).
- **Aqara Climate Sensor W100**, Thread/Matter or Zigbee: 3.4" LCD, temperature and humidity, claimed ±0.2 °C / ±2 % RH (manufacturer figures), three buttons ([Matter Alpha](https://www.matteralpha.com/review/aqara-w100-climate-sensor-review-a-great-but-unfinished-piece-for-matter), [Micro Center](https://www.microcenter.com/product/709337/climate-sensor-w100)).
- **AirGradient ONE**, Wi-Fi, core HA integration (Works with Home Assistant): CO₂, PM, VOC/NOx, temperature, humidity; small 1.3" OLED. Price not verified ([HA docs](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/airgradient/), [SmartHomeScene](https://smarthomescene.com/reviews/airgradient-one-review-and-esphome-integration/)).
- **IKEA TIMMERFLOTTE**, $9.99, Matter over Thread: temperature/humidity, display shows on button press only; mixed accuracy reports and one report of weekly drop-offs ([IKEA US](https://www.ikea.com/us/en/p/timmerflotte-temperature-humidity-sensor-smart-50618957/), [T3](https://www.t3.com/home-living/smart-home/ikea-timmerflotte-temperature-and-humidity-sensor-review-a-budget-friendly-way-to-monitor-your-home-environment)).

**Aaron leans toward the AirGradient ONE (2026-11-22).** It costs USD 230 assembled or USD 138 as a pre-soldered kit with the same hardware but no warranty or test report ([AirGradient](https://www.airgradient.com/indoor/), as fetched 2026-11-22). Inside: SenseAir S8 or S88 for CO₂, Plantower PMS5003 for PM, Sensirion SGP41 for VOC and NOx, Sensirion SHT40 for temperature and humidity, on an ESP32-C3. Power is USB-C and needs a 5 V 2 A USB-A plug, which isn't included. Wi-Fi is 2.4 GHz only. "Ayo Rivah" broadcasts 2.4, 5 and 6 GHz with WPA2/WPA3 transition and PMF optional (checked via `unifi-api rest/wlanconf`), so the ESP32-C3 can join. HA polls it locally every minute and can auto-discover it; that needs firmware 3.1.1+ for the local API. With Configuration source set to Local, HA exposes Display brightness, LED bar brightness and a Cloud connection switch; turning cloud off keeps the local connection ([HA docs](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/airgradient/)).

**The kit is easy to build** ([assembly guide](https://www.airgradient.com/documentation/one-v9/)). There's no soldering; the only tool is a Torx T6 driver, which is included, and AirGradient puts it at about 15 minutes. You plug three sensor modules into labelled sockets (the PM sensor comes pre-mounted), close the case with four screws, and the firmware comes pre-installed. Gotchas: the SHT4x and SGP41 modules look alike and swapping them gives wrong readings; note the PMS serial number before closing the case; CO₂ automatic baseline calibration takes about 7 days.

**Placement** ([AirGradient](https://www.airgradient.com/documentation/kb/mount-airgradient-one)): it can go on a wall or a desk, using the two included stands. On a wall, mount it 150–250 cm up (never below 100 cm or above 300 cm) with M4 screws through the left and right holes, which line up with US junction boxes. Keep it at least 90 cm from any HVAC outlet, out of direct sun, away from doors, windows, hot exterior walls and large electronics, and leave clear air above and below it. For Poppy's room Claude added two points: keep it out of the Canopy humidifier's plume, and tack the cable down, since a wall mount means a 2 m USB cable running down to an outlet.

**Aranet4 Home** (asked the same day). NDIR CO₂ is rated ±(30 ppm + 3 %) over 0–9999 ppm and temperature ±0.3 °C ([Aranet](https://aranet.com/products/aranet4/)). Aranet publishes no humidity tolerance. It also reads pressure, has no PM or VOC, runs on 2× AA for up to about 4 years, and has an unlit e-ink screen. About USD 250 per [HouseFresh](https://housefresh.com/aranet4-home-sensor-review/); not checked at a retailer. HA's core [Aranet integration](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/aranet/) is Local Push over Bluetooth, needs firmware 1.2.0+ and the "Smart Home integration" toggle in the Aranet Home app, and auto-discovers once Bluetooth works. Blocker here: HA has no working Bluetooth. HA's [Bluetooth docs](https://www.home-assistant.io/connectivity/bluetooth/) recommend an ESPHome Bluetooth proxy for virtualised installs over USB passthrough; a CSR8510A10 USB adapter passed to the HAOS VM is the alternative.
