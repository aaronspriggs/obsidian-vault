## Goal

Put a 21.5" Retina 4K iMac shell on top of the 30U rack as a 4096×2304 monitor: its own panel and case driven by an aftermarket board, with a display input and the original power button working. Speakers are wanted but optional (Aaron, 2026-11-20). Aaron is treating it as a tinkering project.

## Status (2026-11-20)

Researched, nothing bought. The donor, board and layout are chosen (below). What it will display, and from which source, is open.

## Plan

**Donor:** a 2017 21.5" Retina 4K iMac (iMac18,2, panel LM215UH1 (SD)(B1)). Candidate: eBay seller Second Life Technology, i7-7700 / 16 GB / 1 TB Fusion / Radeon Pro 560, US $189.99 ($179.99 with coupon), 3 available. Before buying, get an About This Mac screenshot showing "Retina 4K" and confirm Find My is off. Apple lists the 21.5" at 52.8 cm wide, 45.0 cm tall with the stand, 17.5 cm stand depth, 5.66 kg ([specs](https://support.apple.com/en-us/112026)).

**Board:** AliExpress "For iMac A1418 21.5" 4K LM215UH1 Control Drive Board SDA1/SDB1/SPA2", silkscreen R9513 V4, $87.50 (4.8 stars, 26 sold, seen 2026-11-20). Inputs: 2× HDMI, 2× DP, 3.5 mm jack, 12 V barrel; it has a 4-pin speaker amp header (Aaron confirmed from the listing). The MacRumors DIY 5K thread calls the right chip for 21.5" 4K "R9153"; one of the two names is a transposition. It does not list the 2019 panel (SD B2), so the donor must be a 2015 or 2017. Questions for the seller: which panel cable ships (the 2015 and 2017 panels use different connectors, per [iFixit](https://ts.ifixit.com/Answers/History/673833)); whether DP and HDMI do 4096×2304 at 60 Hz; whether the 3.5 mm jack is an output; whether it supports DDC/CI (brightness from the source machine).

**Power:** 12 V, 8 A, 5.5×2.1 mm center-positive per a [Qarequ listing](https://qarequ.com/products/lcd-driver-board-for-lm215uh1-sda1-sdb1-spa1-spa2) for the same board; a MacRumors user recommends 8-10 A over 5 A for brightness. Use an external 12 V ≥8 A brick (e.g. Mean Well GST120A12; check its plug). The thread's first post says 21.5" 4K conversions have no room for an internal PSU. With the USB hub adding up to about 20 W, prefer a brick at the top of the range (10 A) or measure first. Measure real draw with an inline meter once running; it goes on a surge-only PDU outlet or the PDU, not a UPS battery outlet (none spare).

**Rear port map (draft, 2026-11-20):** every original opening gets a job, mounted on a printed carrier that screws onto the old logic board's standoffs, so connectors sit where the original ports were (measure port positions against the screw holes on the removed logic board before selling it). AC inlet: DC jack plus a DisplayPort panel mount (main input; DP rather than USB-C-to-DP for the RTX 3080 Ti workstation, since that cable failed on an RTX 3080 in [aiwipro's 27" build](https://github.com/aiwipro/5K-iMac-Studio-Display-Stock-Look)). Thunderbolt 3 opening 1: USB-C video input through a USB4 extension and a bidirectional USB-C-to-DP cable to the board's second DP input (aiwipro's method). Thunderbolt 3 opening 2: USB-C upstream for the USB hub. 4× USB-A: panel-mount extensions from an internal USB 3 hub board, powered by a 12-to-5 V buck converter (the board has no USB of its own). Ethernet: HDMI panel mount to the board's HDMI input, if an HDMI female fits the RJ45 opening (measure; otherwise file it). Headphone: 3.5 mm extension from the board's jack. SD slot: the button strip's ribbon. Kensington slot: unused.

**Printed parts (MK4, PETG or ASA):** the I/O carrier above; a removable plate in the AC inlet opening, held by keystone-style squeeze latches, carrying the DC jack and the DP panel mount; a collar for a 3.5 mm extension in the old headphone opening; a button pod under the chin, clear of the bottom air intake, with the button strip's ribbon routed out through the SD slot. Claude drafts these in parametric OpenSCAD once measured.

**Prior builds to borrow from (checked 2026-11-20):** no published dimensions exist for the A1418's rear openings or logic board port positions, so measuring stays necessary. [MakerWorld 2549010](https://www.makerworld.com/en/models/2549010), by xstranger, is a finished 2017 A1418 4K build (text and comments read from Aaron's screenshots 2026-11-20): an AliExpress 4K A1418 board on a printed `Mainboard.stl` adapter, fixed right of the fan tube on the two stand-hinge bolts (swapped for longer ones), crossovers on the matching bolts to its left, cables behind the fan tube; the **original PSU kept in its mount**, with a bought mating plug on its 6-pin harness (three +12 V, three ground) feeding the stock fan, a DC PWM fan controller (knob on the rear housing) and the display board; the PSU runs as soon as it's plugged in, no modification. The original fan tube reused with the heat pipes removed. USB openings slightly widened for cable passthrough, finished with a printed TPU gasket. Control buttons moved onto the stand. The iMac power button left unused; the designer says it only signalled the logic board and could be soldered in parallel with the board's power button. One commenter (PortoLAB, 2026-10-27) got a high-pitched whine from the original PSU with all lines reading 12 V and suspected too little load; the designer runs iMac PSUs in three monitors without it. A reviewer printed at 0.12 mm, 3 walls, 25% infill. Next: compare the designer's AliExpress board link with the R9513 listing; if it's the same board, use his `Mainboard.stl`. His nine STLs (Aaron uploaded them 2026-11-20; on the VM at `~/project-files/imac-rack-display/xstranger-21in-4k-makerworld-2549010/`, measured with `stl_holes.py` one level up) give the A1418's anchor points: `Mainboard.stl` is a 100 × 114 × 5.7 mm plate holding the driver board on four M3 holes in a **56 × 106 mm** rectangle (6.6 mm bosses) and fixing to the case through two 4.5 mm holes **24.0 mm apart**, recessed for the stand-hinge bolt heads; `Crossovers.stl` uses a second bolt pair, also 24.0 mm apart. `Controls.stl` (126 × 34 × 12.5 mm) holds a 7-button strip on the stand with `Buttons.stl` caps; `PWM.stl` is the fan-knob box (not needed with automatic control); `WiresHolder.stl` is a strap with M3 holes 113 mm apart; `CameraMount.stl`, `Plug.stl` and `HolesCovers.stl` are small fillers. Whether his `Mainboard.stl` fits the R9513 depends on its mounting holes matching 56 × 106 mm; ask the seller or measure on arrival. KevDoy's 27" 2014 set on Printables](https://www.printables.com/model/311111-apple-imac-27-2014-5k-monitor-conversion) has an I/O door replacing the RAM door, mounted with the original screws (27" only; also blocks Claude). A community [iFixit guide](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Turn+Your+iMac+2015+into+a+monitor/181638) (a 27" Retina) reused original openings: HDMI, DP and camera cables through two USB openings joined into one, audio out through the Ethernet opening, and the power button's own two wires, kept from the bottom left, soldered to the control module's power button pins. Its author dropped the speakers on crossovers as "too nasal".

**Layout model Aaron likes (2026-11-20):** Marco Markl's [27" 2017 5K mounting system](https://makerworld.com/en/models/1390804-apple-imac-27-2017-conversion-to-5k-monitor) (read from Aaron's screenshots; MakerWorld blocks Claude): hex-vented printed cages for the driver board, controller and power supply on arms bolted to the original logic-board mounting points, edge cable ducts and a port cover, with no drilling. Notes from it: the power button is wired by splitting the controller's ribbon cable and joining the iMac button's two wires to its GND and POWER wires (labelled on the board), which keeps the controller's own button working; a special screw under the original logic board is reused for a bracket; the fan was "not strictly necessary" and noisy; speaker sound on crossovers "moderate at best"; PETG, ABS or ASA, with supports on the driver-board and controller parts. Our 21.5" parts follow the same style, anchored on the measured logic-board standoffs.
Measured from his STLs (Aaron uploaded nine plus the USDZ, 2026-11-20; kept on the ClaudeCode VM at `~/project-files/imac-rack-display/markl-27in-5k-makerworld-1390804/`, not backed up and re-downloadable from MakerWorld; the measuring script is `stl_analyze.py` one level up): walls 2.0 mm (some 1.5 mm); hex vents 6.5 mm across flats (7.5 mm across corners); feet are 10.3 mm rings with 7.3 mm bores over the iMac's mounting points; M3 holes at 3.0 mm (self-tapping) and 3.4 mm (clearance), M2 at about 2.1 mm for the crossovers. Envelopes: driver-board cage 146 × 171 × 41 mm, controller-and-fan 135 × 125 × 40 mm, power-supply cage 174 × 112 × 24 mm (all 27"; the 21.5" case is shallower, so ours will be lower). The port cover is a 2.4 mm plate 123 × 19 mm with four 3.8 × 11.7 mm openings in a row plus a 25 × 1.7 mm slot; check whether the 21.5" port row matches before reusing its spacing. Cable ducts are 30 × 30 mm clips with a 6.8 × 12.4 mm channel.

**Housing layout model (2026-11-20, planning only):** built from iFixit's full-resolution photos, pulled through the iFixit guide API: the head-on teardown shot ([teardown 92170](https://www.ifixit.com/Teardown/iMac+Intel+21.5-Inch+Retina+4K+Display+2017+Teardown/92170) step 4, 6079 × 4559 px) as the master, the board-lift shot from the [logic board guide 95631](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/iMac+Intel+21.5-Inch+Retina+4K+Display+(2017)+Logic+Board+Replacement/95631) for what the fan, drive and board hide, and the bare-board shot (teardown step 6) for port positions. Coordinates are mm in front view with the display removed: X from the case centre, right positive (mirror it from the back); Y up from the case bottom edge. **Model checked against xstranger's STLs (2026-11-20):** the 24.0 mm bolt spacing was one of the inputs for scaling the model, so it can't confirm anything. These checks are independent of it:

- His `Mainboard.stl` plate, put on the right bolt pair and run outward, covers X 52 to 151 and Y 107 to 221. That fills the vacated logic-board area, 8 mm clear of the fan duct and 4 mm clear of the right speaker's lower edge. That is tight enough to suggest the model's duct, hinge and speaker positions agree with his real build, but the margins are inside the ±3 to 5 mm tolerance, so it doesn't prove them.
- His `Crossovers.stl`, put on the left pair and run outward, sits in the empty HDD bay at X −149 to −52, Y 155 to 195. It is 17 mm below the blower and 7 mm clear of the duct.
- His SD-slot cover in `HolesCovers.stl` is 26.4 mm long. The board photo gives the SD slot as about 27 mm.
- `Plug.stl` is a 41.5 × 13.3 mm plug with a 1 mm lip. It most likely fills the four USB-A openings that he widened into one. My port centres give about 45 mm for that run, so either the openings are narrower than the 5.5 mm I assumed or the pitch reads about 1 mm high. Check at teardown.
- `CameraMount.stl` has screw holes 41.4 mm apart. The photo shows no clear screw heads at the camera, so this was not checked.

Scale cross-check, same day: Aaron asked whether a print dimension could set the grid instead. The two photos were already scaled independently. One used xstranger's 24.0 mm bolt pair. The other used the 528 mm case width plus the 2.5" drive. Three standoffs are visible in both photos, and the bolt-scaled distances between them come out within 0.2 mm, 0.1 mm and 2.8 mm of the case-scaled ones. The last pair has one ring partly hidden. Scaling everything from the bolts instead would put the case at 528.5 mm wide, a 0.1% change. So no rescale: the print, Apple's case width and the drive all agree to within about 0.5%. What's left in the error budget is picking points by eye on the photos (about 1–2 mm) and parts sitting at different depths.

Both plates are drawn in the model files, as features 23 to 25.

**Wiring:** the iMac power button's two wires joined to the GND and POWER wires of the button strip's ribbon (Markl's method); speakers on the 4-pin header, simplest in series without crossovers ([jorycz](https://github.com/jorycz/iMac-5K-display-convert) lists the 27" speaker pinout and a crossover option); set the OSD before sealing.

**Budget:** donor $190 + board $88 + brick ~$30 + extensions, adhesive and wire ~$40. Selling the donor's logic board, i7-7700, RAM, PSU and drives (estimated $100-150 net) and the 2011 iMac (estimated $80-150, boxed) covers most of it.

## Decisions

- **2026-11-20 (Aaron): 21.5", not 27".** The 21.5" is about the rack's width on top; the 27" (65 cm) would overhang. A 27" 5K conversion stays a possible desk project.
- **2026-11-20 (Aaron): Retina 4K donor, not the 2011 he owns.** The 2011 (MC812LL/A, 1080p, magnetic glass) was the cheaper build, with LM215WF3 kits at $30-60 and Juicy Crumb's DockLite at about $300; Aaron chose the sharper 4K panel and is comfortable opening glued iMacs from a past job. The 2011 gets sold whole with its box rather than parted out. Upgrading the 2011 instead (i7-2600S, 16 GB, SSD, Kepler GPU, about $210-330) was rejected: about a third of an M1's CPU for M1-mini money.
- **2026-11-20 (Aaron): must-haves are a display input and the original power button;** speakers optional; camera, USB hub and Ethernet dropped.
- **2026-11-20 (Aaron): reuse as many rear openings as possible,** rather than one loose cable hole. Draft port map above; it supersedes the earlier plan of two video inputs through both Thunderbolt openings.
- **2026-11-20: not two video ports in the AC inlet.** Nobody has published the inlet opening's size, and two screw-ear panel mounts need about 60 mm; the Thunderbolt and Ethernet openings carry the other inputs instead.
- **2026-11-20: external 12 V brick rather than USB-C power.** USB-C would need a 20 V PD trigger plus a 20-to-12 V buck converter inside a case with no room for a PSU; reconsider only if measured draw is well under 60 W.

## Open questions

- Power: the MakerWorld build shows the original PSU can stay, but Aaron (2026-11-20) is wary of the whine one builder reported and leans to the external 12 V brick; Claude's alternative is a Mean Well LRS-150-12 inside, wired to the Apple AC inlet (stock cord), if it fits the old PSU's space. Decide before ordering. Aaron found end brackets for the LRS-200 case ([MakerWorld 2305588](https://makerworld.com/en/models/2305588), copy at `~/project-files/imac-rack-display/meanwell-lrs-200-brackets/`): they fit an LRS-200-12 (same case, 215 × 115 × 30 mm) but not the smaller LRS-150-12, and the -24 version is the wrong voltage for this 12 V board.
- Cooling: Aaron doesn't want a manual fan knob (2026-11-20). Proposed: keep the stock blower and its intake-bottom, exhaust-behind-stand path, driven by a temperature-controlled 4-wire PWM module with an NTC probe on the board heatsink; a Noctua 40 mm on the heatsink only if testing shows hot spots. Apple fans swap 12 V and ground against the standard pinout (aiwipro).
- Board: xstranger's AliExpress link opens a MacPanel Store listing (LM215UH1 SDA1/SDB1/SDC1 variants, $83.26, 1 review at 1.0 stars, 2 sold, seen 2026-11-20), not the R9513 listing; his `Mainboard.stl` fits his board. Compare the two boards' mounting holes before choosing.

- What it displays and from which machine (a server console through a KVM, a dashboard source, or a Mac). The rack plan has room for a possible Mac Studio, not one in hand.
- Measurements, taken at teardown: AC inlet opening size and edge thickness, recess depth, clearance to the stand hinge, how the inlet is held, Thunderbolt, USB-A and Ethernet opening sizes and spacing, port positions against the screw holes on the removed logic board, SD slot size, chin underside and intake slot, the original PSU's footprint and height and the gap from rear housing to panel there (an LRS-150-12 is about 159 × 97 × 30 mm; the photo model shows only about 200 × 58 mm of PSU board above the chin line, so it fits only if the bay runs well behind the chin), speaker connector and impedance.
- The rack's outside width on top, once the 30U rack arrives.
- Whether the board restores its input after a power cut.
