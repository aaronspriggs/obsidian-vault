## Goal

Put a 21.5" Retina 4K iMac shell on top of the 30U rack as a 4096×2304 monitor: its own panel and case driven by an aftermarket board, with a display input and the original power button working. Speakers are wanted but optional (Aaron, 2026-11-20). A tinkering project.

## Status (2026-11-20)

Researched, nothing bought. The donor, board and a VESA-mounted layout for a monitor arm are chosen (below).

## Plan

**Donor:** a 2017 21.5" Retina 4K iMac (iMac18,2, panel LM215UH1 (SD)(B1)). Candidate: eBay seller Second Life Technology, i7-7700 / 16 GB / 1 TB Fusion / Radeon Pro 560, US $189.99 ($179.99 with coupon), 3 available. Before buying, get an About This Mac screenshot showing "Retina 4K"; confirm Find My is off. Apple lists the 21.5" at 52.8 cm wide, 45.0 cm tall with the stand, 17.5 cm stand depth, 5.66 kg ([specs](https://support.apple.com/en-us/112026)).

**Board:** AliExpress "For iMac A1418 21.5" 4K LM215UH1 Control Drive Board SDA1/SDB1/SPA2", silkscreen R9513 V4, $87.50 (4.8 stars, 26 sold, seen 2026-11-20). Inputs: 2× HDMI, 2× DP, 3.5 mm jack, 12 V barrel; it has a 4-pin speaker amp header (Aaron confirmed from the listing). The MacRumors DIY 5K thread calls the right chip for 21.5" 4K "R9153"; one of the two names is a transposition. It does not list the 2019 panel (SD B2), so the donor must be a 2015 or 2017. Questions for the seller: which panel cable ships (the 2015 and 2017 panels use different connectors, per [iFixit](https://ts.ifixit.com/Answers/History/673833)); whether DP and HDMI do 4096×2304 at 60 Hz; whether the 3.5 mm jack is an output; whether it supports DDC/CI (brightness from the source machine); the board's outline, mounting-hole pattern and tallest part, which size the printed carrier.

**Power:** 12 V, 8 A, 5.5×2.1 mm center-positive per a [Qarequ listing](https://qarequ.com/products/lcd-driver-board-for-lm215uh1-sda1-sdb1-spa1-spa2) for the same board; a MacRumors user recommends 8-10 A over 5 A for brightness. Board up to about 96 W, hub up to about 20 W, fan a few W: use a Mean Well GST160A12-R7B (12 V 11.5 A, 138 W; [Mean Well spec](https://www.meanwell.com/Upload/PDF/GST160A/GST160A-SPEC.PDF)). Its 4-pin locking DIN plug carries 11.5 A better than a 5.5×2.1 barrel, so the inlet plate gets a matching 4-pin panel jack, wired inside to the board, buck and fan controller (confirm the jack part against the spec). Measure real draw with an inline meter once running; it goes on a surge-only PDU outlet or the PDU, not a UPS battery outlet (none spare).

**VESA mount and build layout (2026-11-20):** Apple's VESA Mount Adapter Kit replaces the stand. Its plate goes inside on the four stand-hinge screws, the tongue through the stand slot, a 100 × 100 mm bracket outside. Buy the 2017 set (plate 923-01625, tongue 923-01674, bracket 923-0421) and confirm the part numbers with the seller, since the 2015 plate and tongue differ. Used kits: beetstech, $86.15. It needs a Pentalobe P15 driver. Those hinge screws then carry the whole display, so nothing printed goes on them. The R9513 sits on a printed carrier on the logic-board standoffs. Tall parts stay in X 70–120, Y 100–250, where the calibrated model has 20.4 mm or more free; only flat parts go past X 120. A temperature-controlled fan PWM module goes in the old PSU bay (X −135 to −45). The AC inlet carries a printed plate with the 4-pin power jack and a DisplayPort panel mount (main input; USB-C-to-DP failed on an RTX 3080 in [aiwipro's build](https://github.com/aiwipro/5K-iMac-Studio-Display-Stock-Look)). It sits below the bracket, so cables drop out under the arm. USB hub: a USB 3 hub board on a printed tray in the empty HDD bay (X −135 to −66, Y 120–205, 19.4 mm or more free), fed by a 12-to-5 V buck beside the fan controller, upstream USB-C through one Thunderbolt opening (to the same source machine as the video), USB-A to the four USB-A openings. Those openings sit about 13.1 mm apart and the Thunderbolt pair 10.4 mm (photo, ±1), bare-receptacle spacing, so molded extension ends won't fit side by side; the port block options are in the change note (2026-11-20). Other openings: a USB-C video input through the second Thunderbolt opening, HDMI through Ethernet if it fits, 3.5 mm audio through the headphone opening, the button strip's ribbon through the SD slot. A button pod goes under the chin, clear of the intake. Prints: MK4, PETG or ASA, parametric OpenSCAD. Positions, depth checks, arm load and the drawing (`layout/`) are in the change note below.

**Prior builds to borrow from (checked 2026-11-20):** nobody has published the A1418's rear openings or port positions, so measuring stays necessary. [MakerWorld 2549010](https://www.makerworld.com/en/models/2549010), by xstranger, is a finished 2017 A1418 4K build. It keeps the **original PSU in its mount**, feeding the board, the stock fan and a PWM fan controller through a mating plug on its 6-pin harness (three +12 V, three ground), unmodified, and reuses the fan tube with the heat pipes removed. One commenter (PortoLAB, 2026-10-27) got a high-pitched whine from that PSU and suspected too little load. His `Mainboard.stl` and `Crossovers.stl` mount on the hinge bolts and his `Controls.stl` on the stand, so the VESA layout can't use them. His full notes, STLs (`~/project-files/imac-rack-display/xstranger-21in-4k-makerworld-2549010/`) and two 27" builds are in the change note below.

**Layout style Aaron likes (2026-11-20):** Marco Markl's [27" 2017 5K mounting system](https://makerworld.com/en/models/1390804-apple-imac-27-2017-conversion-to-5k-monitor): hex-vented printed cages on arms bolted to the original logic-board mounting points, no drilling. Ours follow it on the measured standoffs; his notes and STL measurements are in the change note below.

**Housing model (2026-11-20, planning only):** plan-view and depth model of the A1418 housing from iFixit's photos, xstranger's STLs and the dimensions.com drawing; sources, fit checks, the VESA layout and files are in [the change note](<../changes/2026-11-20 Modelled the A1418 housing in plan and depth from iFixit photos and public drawings.md>), files on the VM at `~/project-files/imac-rack-display/a1418-housing-model/` with STLs.

Plan view ±3 mm at the centre, ±5 mm at the edges; mm from the case centre, right positive, Y up from the bottom edge. Hinge bolt pairs X ±57, Y 163 and 187; logic-board standoffs (69, 314), (178, 252), (50, 72), (165, 83); rear ports Y 45–47; old PSU board X −155 to 46, Y 65 to 123. Depth uses LG's 11.05 mm display assembly and a skin fitted so the stock PSU, HDD and blower clear: a 43.6 mm case, inside the drawings' 38.8–44.3 mm. That assumes a 16 mm PSU tall zone, inferred; photos and listings say 20–25 mm. Calibrated free depth: 31 mm at the hinge, 21–24 mm across the old PSU bay, 13–17 mm along the chin. The sides (outside about X ±120) are unvalidated because no speaker depth is published. One caliper pass at the points in the change note settles the PSU, speakers, panel and wall.

**Wiring:** the iMac power button's two wires joined to the GND and POWER wires of the button strip's ribbon (Markl's method); speakers on the 4-pin header in series without crossovers ([jorycz](https://github.com/jorycz/iMac-5K-display-convert) lists the 27" speaker pinout and a crossover option); set the OSD before sealing.

**Budget:** donor $190 + board $88 + VESA kit ~$86 + P15 driver ~$10 + GST160A12-R7B brick (price not checked) + extensions, adhesive and wire ~$40, plus the arm. Selling the donor's board, CPU, RAM, PSU and drives ($100-150 net) and the boxed 2011 ($80-150) covers most of it.

## Decisions

- **2026-11-20 (Aaron): 21.5", not 27".** The 21.5" is about the rack's width on top; the 27" (65 cm) would overhang. A 27" 5K conversion stays a possible desk project.
- **2026-11-20 (Aaron): Retina 4K donor, not the 2011 he owns.** The 2011 (MC812LL/A, 1080p) was cheaper to convert (LM215WF3 kits $30-60, Juicy Crumb DockLite about $300), but Aaron chose the sharper 4K panel and is comfortable opening glued iMacs. The 2011 is sold whole with its box. Upgrading it instead (i7-2600S, 16 GB, SSD, about $210-330) was rejected: a third of an M1 for M1-mini money.
- **2026-11-20 (Aaron): must-haves are a display input and the original power button;** speakers optional; camera and Ethernet dropped. A USB hub is wanted if it fits (Aaron, later the same day, reversing the earlier drop).
- **2026-11-20 (Aaron): external 12 V brick, not the stock PSU.** An internal supply would be nice but isn't needed; the LRS-150 doesn't fit (change note) and the stock PSU risks the whine one builder reported. Aaron's LRS-200 end brackets ([MakerWorld 2305588](https://makerworld.com/en/models/2305588)) aren't needed.
- **2026-11-20 (Aaron): VESA mount on a monitor arm, not the stand. (Claude): Apple's VESA kit, not a clamp adapter.** Clamp adapters on the stock stand foot (VIVO STAND-MACB, Tether Tools Rock Solid) need no teardown and keep xstranger's hinge-bolt plates usable, but they put the stand's weight, depth and lever arm on the arm. We remove the logic board anyway, which is the hard part of fitting Apple's kit. Consequences (board on the standoffs, xstranger's plates out, speakers in series) are in the layout above.
- **2026-11-20 (Aaron): reuse as many rear openings as possible,** rather than one loose cable hole. The port map (in the layout above) supersedes the earlier plan of two video inputs through both Thunderbolt openings.
- **2026-11-20: not two video ports in the AC inlet.** Nobody has published the inlet opening's size, and two screw-ear panel mounts need about 60 mm; the Thunderbolt and Ethernet openings carry the other inputs instead.
- **2026-11-20: external 12 V brick rather than USB-C power.** USB-C would need a 20 V PD trigger plus a 20-to-12 V buck converter inside a case with no room for a PSU; reconsider only if measured draw is well under 60 W.
- **2026-11-20 (Claude): the depth model is checked against the parts Apple ships.** The dimensions.com views are silhouettes that only bound the rear surface, so averaging them (38.8 and 44.3 mm) was rejected. The display thickness comes from LG's spec for the A1418 assembly, and the skin is deepened, with its 5 mm edge fixed, until the stock PSU, HDD and blower fit. LG's unlaminated-panel thickness (13.5 mm) was rejected. The stock PSU's 16 mm tall zone is inferred from the case depth, against 20–25 mm from photos and listings. It is the weakest input and the first thing to measure.

## Open questions

- Cooling: Aaron doesn't want a manual fan knob (2026-11-20). Proposed: keep the stock blower and its intake-bottom, exhaust-behind-stand path, driven by a temperature-controlled 4-wire PWM module with an NTC probe on the board heatsink; a Noctua 40 mm on the heatsink only if testing shows hot spots. Apple fans swap 12 V and ground against the standard pinout (aiwipro).
- Board: xstranger's AliExpress link opens a MacPanel Store listing (LM215UH1 SDA1/SDB1/SDC1 variants, $83.26, 1 review at 1.0 stars, 2 sold, seen 2026-11-20), not the R9513 listing; his `Mainboard.stl` fits his board. Compare the two boards' mounting holes before choosing.

- How the arm attaches to the rack top: Aaron is checking whether a clamp works or it needs something special (2026-11-20). That, and the weighed unit (est. 4 to 4.5 kg, at most 5.66 kg), choose the arm, whose rated range must include roughly 3.5 to 6 kg.
- What it displays and from which machine (a server console through a KVM, a dashboard source, or a Mac). The rack plan has room for a possible Mac Studio, not one in hand.
- Measurements, taken at teardown: AC inlet opening size and edge thickness, recess depth, clearance to the stand hinge, how the inlet is held, Thunderbolt, USB-A and Ethernet opening sizes and spacing, port positions against the screw holes on the removed logic board, SD slot size, chin underside and intake slot, speaker connector and impedance. For the depth model: rim-plane-to-inner-rear-wall depth at the ten points listed in the change note, display assembly thickness (centre and edge), stock PSU height at the bulk capacitors and transformer, blower height and one speaker's depth.
- The rack's outside width on top, once the 30U rack arrives.
- Whether the board restores its input after a power cut.
